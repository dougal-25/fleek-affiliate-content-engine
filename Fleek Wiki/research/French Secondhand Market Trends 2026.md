# French Secondhand Market Trends 2026

> The French market lens — where the FR secondhand/friperie market is heading and how French buyers now discover and choose resale sellers. The macro backdrop the whole engine is being launched into. Sourced 2026-07-09.
Part of [[index]] · siblings: [[FR Resale Platform Landscape]], [[French Reseller Community Sentiment]], [[FR Creator Content Formats]]

### Key facts — market shape

- **Vinted is now the largest clothing retailer in France** — surpassed Amazon and Zara; FR secondhand market grew **~140% in two years**, driven by inflation + climate concern — [France24 / YT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IxP4V6IcWDk), [Keith Christiansen](https://keithchristiansen.substack.com/p/second-hand-france)
- **Luxury resale is the only profitable subgroup**: +35% in 2025; **27% of the luxury market is now circular** — general secondhand runs net losses despite volume — [Bank & Vogue](https://www.bankvogue.com/blog/closing-2025-market-trends-shaping-the-global-secondhand-wholesale-industry/)
- Global resale projected to hit **$317B by 2028**; EU **EPR legislation** is accelerating resale mandates FR is implementing — [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/circular-secondhand-fashion-resale-booming/)
- Format map: **P2P apps** (Vinted, Leboncoin) · **curated** (Selency) · **kilo shops** (Kilo Shop, Kawaii Nantes) · **offline** (brocantes, vide-greniers, spring/fall)

### Key facts — consumer behaviour shift (2026)

- **Discovery is going social-first**: for Gen Z/Millennials, **TikTok Shop + Instagram** are now primary discovery channels, ahead of physical flea markets — [sourceready](https://www.sourceready.com/report/detail/france-fast-fashion-market-report-2026)
- **Selection = authenticity + identity, not just price**: buyers want authenticated provenance (Chanel launched resale platform **Nevold**) and *unique* pieces as identity markers — rejecting the "mid-range" — [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/france-luxury-goods-market)
- **"Archive" is replacing "vintage"** as the Gen Z-appealing frame — [IG](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DQoIhcvDDKo/?hl=en)
- **Regulatory tailwind**: 2026 anti-fast-fashion laws + environmental penalties push consumers off Shein/Zara/H&M toward circular platforms — [sourceready](https://www.sourceready.com/report/detail/france-fast-fashion-market-report-2026)
- **Economic polarisation**: households cutting non-essentials hard (electronics −55pts, sport −52pts) → into secondhand/repair — [intotheminds](https://www.intotheminds.com/blog/en/consumer-trends-2026/)
- **Experiential vintage fairs** now draw 3,000–15,000 visitors/edition (DJ sets, customisation workshops); the 18–35 group treats thrifting as ecological statement — [keyqo](https://keyqo.io/en/blog/vintage-fairs-france-guide-2026)
- Traditional flea markets feel "quieter" (fuel prices + sellers moving online) — [YT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b6vrjKOOFVA)

### 2026-07-09 deep-read enrichment — the numbers (institutional anchors)

**Market size** (mind the scope split — all-goods vs fashion-only):
- **Fashion/textile secondhand: €6bn (2022)** — Institut Français de la Mode (IFM), the defensible hard anchor.
- **All-goods secondhand: ~€14bn (≈2023–24), doubled 2019→2024** — Xerfi.
- Secondhand = **10.9% of clothing purchases by value (Q1 2025), 16.3% among 18–34s** — IFM. "1 garment in 10 bought in France is secondhand."
- Growth ~15–25%/yr depending on scope; global resale $128bn (2024) → ~$350bn (2028).

**Who + why:**
- **~67% of French buy or sell secondhand**; ~75% bought in the past year; 44% buy at least monthly — iligo (Apr 2025) / enov (Jul 2026).
- **18–34s are the growth engine**; committed profile = female 25–34; couples-with-children 30% of buyers.
- **Price dominates, ecology is fading**: 79% of buyers motivated by savings; ecological motivation **dropped 8pts (buyers) / 28pts (sellers) vs 2024** (iligo). Buyers expect ~30% discount vs new. ← important: pitch *savings + stock*, not eco-halo.
- Channel split: 64% buy via specialist platforms (Vinted/Vestiaire), 55% generalist (Leboncoin), 50% physical.

**Regulation — the tailwind, dated:**
- **Anti-fast-fashion law ("loi Shein") — definitively adopted 29 June 2026.** Progressive **malus up to €20/item by 2030** (cap 50% of pre-tax price). **Targets Shein, Temu, AliExpress; explicitly spares Zara, Kiabi, Primark.** Ad ban uncertain under EU law — fallback restricts **influencers** from promoting these platforms. Doesn't regulate resale directly but **structurally advantages it.** — [publicsenat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/politique/loi-anti-fast-fashion-le-senat-adopte-definitivement-le-texte-penalisant-les-produits-shein-et-temu)
- **Loi AGEC (2020) + textile EPR** via **Refashion** eco-organism; producers pay an éco-contribution. Sorting sector **in crisis** — €49m (2025) + €57m (2026) state emergency aid → margin pressure in the bale-supply chain.

**Platforms (hard numbers):**
- **Vinted 2025: GMV €10.8bn (+47%), revenue €1.1bn (+38%), net profit €62m**, >100M customers, ~€8bn valuation — official newsroom (Apr 2026). **France = #1 market, >23M registered users (~⅓ of the population).**
- **Vinted is #1 clothing seller in France by volume — ahead of Amazon (#2), Kiabi (#3), Shein (#5)** (IFM). Resale platforms = 15% of online fashion revenue.
- Leboncoin: >88M listings, €27bn exchanged (2024). Vestiaire: ~€160m revenue, ~1.2M FR users.

### Why this matters for the case study
France is a **structurally favourable launch market**: the biggest retailer is already a resale platform (Vinted, €10.8bn GMV), a just-passed law taxes fast-fashion and even restricts influencers from promoting Shein/Temu (pushing creators toward *resale-native* offers like Fleek), discovery has moved to the exact social channels a creator engine operates on, and buyers select on price + uniqueness. The macro wind is at Fleek's back — this is the "why now, why France" slide, now with institutional numbers behind every claim.
