### The grade vocabulary (essential — this is the trade's language)
- **Original** — collected and sold with **NO sorting whatsoever**: all seasons/qualities/genders mixed, may include linens and stray shoes/bags. Cheapest, riskiest tier.
- **Crème / Extra Crème** — top tier: barely-worn to new, all seasons. Premium.
- **Grade A** — like-new, no visible defect (top-of-A often written **A+ CRÈME**).
- **Grade B** — very good, light wear. **Grade C** — acceptable, lowest common graded tier.
- **Balle / ballot** — compressed bale, historic wholesale format, 10–50 kg.
- **Au kilo** vs **à la pièce** — by weight vs by the item. **Box (au kilo triée)** — pre-sorted curated kilo box (beginner-friendly).
- **Tri / trié / 1er choix** — sorting / sorted / first-pick. **Dégriffé** — label removed. **Semi-grossiste** — intermediary letting small resellers buy smaller/cherry-picked lots. **Déballage** — the bale-unboxing content genre. **Colis mystère** — unseen-contents lot.

### The bale economics (the numbers resellers cite)
- **Raw ballot**: 10–50 kg, **€3–8/kg**, unsorted → **€0.80–1.50/piece** before waste, ~**€1–5/piece** after.
- **Graded box (Grade A)**: higher €/kg but **90%+ exploitable**, pre-washed/ironed → **€1–2 per usable piece**.
- **Full market spread**: ~**€1.50–15/kg** across quality tiers.
- **Waste is the hidden cost**: *"20 à 40 % de pièces invendables sur Vinted dans un ballot brut (taches, trous, démodé, hors saison)."*
- **Resale multiple**: vendor pitch x9–12; **realistic beginner x3–5** after unsold stock. *"Sur un article revendu 18 €, ça ne double pas ta marge : ça la triple."*
- **The threshold** — when raw bales beat curated boxes: *"Le ballot devient pertinent quand tu dépasses environ 100 ventes par mois"* (+ space, sorting time, cash-flow shoulders). Below that, graded boxes win once your time is priced in. **150 sales/mo ≈ 5 parcels/day, 300–500 permanent stock** — a full-time métier, not passive.

### The scam red-flags (the outreach ammunition)
The single most-repeated complaint is **"Grade A ordered, Grade B received"** + suppliers going silent. Verbatim on [[French Reseller Community Sentiment]]. Honest-supplier checklist (from ballotsvetements.com):
1. Refuses a factory visit / rushes you.
2. **Opaque packaging** — dark wrap so you can't see contents (dumps wrong-season/poor stock).
3. **Refuses to open a sealed bale of your choosing** — *"Un fournisseur honnête ne refusera jamais d'ouvrir un ballot pour démontrer la qualité."*
4. Price too good to be true.
5. Attractive pieces staged on top of the bale.
How resellers actually pick: transparent packaging, willingness to open a bale, visible SIRET/mentions légales/address, Trustpilot avis, French-collected > mixed imports, **start with graded boxes before raw bales**.

### 2026-07-09 Reddit enrichment — bale ground-truth from practitioners (r/Flipping, r/exmormon; raw in [[reddit_quora_threads_2026-07-09]])

The English resale subs are where the raw bale mechanics get discussed bluntly (French Reddit suppresses business posts). Consistent numbers:
- **Blind bale pricing: $0.10–0.50/lb** (a "1000 lb bale... $0.15–0.20/lb"). A **1000 lb bale ≈ 2000 tees**.
- **Yield is brutal**: *"50-70% are probably gonna be junk"*; *"hope to be able to use at least 1:5 shirts."* One thrift-store volunteer who *makes* the bales: *"For the 1000 lb bale I can pull maybe a small grocery bag worth of high-end, worthwhile clothes."* → matches the FR "20–40% invendable" figure; the raw-bale waste rate is the universal killer.
- **"Sourcing is the ceiling"** (r/Flipping consensus): *"you will always be capped by what you can bring in."* Selling skill can't rescue bad sourcing.
- **Pick-your-own beats sealed bales**: the pro move is negotiating direct access to a collection centre's incoming stream (*"asked if they would let me pick my own bale"*) rather than buying blind.
- **How bale supply actually works** (r/exmormon, plain-English): thrift chains bale unsold donations into **~2000 lb bundles**, sell **by the pound**, often exported as **"rags"** to dodge export duties — the same channel that feeds friperie importers.
- **The reckoning every reseller hits**: *"How much am I getting paid for the time involved?... Getting paid $2 an hour and losing all the family time is not worth it."* Raw bales only pay once you price your sorting time in — reinforces the **cost-per-sellable-piece** rule above.

**Why this matters:** the practitioner numbers independently confirm the vault's economics (waste 50–70%, x3–5 realistic, cost-per-sellable-piece is king) — and every one is a Fleek Sort selling point: pre-graded, trustworthy, low-filler stock removes the exact gamble these threads complain about.
